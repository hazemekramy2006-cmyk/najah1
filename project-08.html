Overview

A safety-critical HCI research project addressing the single most consequential moment of Hajj: the Wuquf (Standing) at Arafat, when a pilgrim's entire Hajj is invalidated if they aren't physically inside an invisible, unmarked boundary. The team designed a smart wristband + companion app that verifies a pilgrim's location offline and communicates safety status purely through touch — no screen, no language, no sound required.

What this project taught me

  • Designing for a "temporary state" persona — not who someone normally is, but who they become under extreme heat, crowd density, and cognitive load.
  • Building an entire interaction language out of haptic patterns alone, for a 150+ language, all-ages, all-literacy-levels audience.
  • Multi-actor coordination design across pilgrim, group leader, Hajj authority, and medical staff.
  • Offline-first safety architecture: designing a system that must work with zero network reliance.

The context

Arafat Plain: a 20 km² zone, 2–3 million pilgrims, 42–48°C heat, 90–100+ dB noise, and a sea of identical tents with no visible landmarks or reliable cellular signal — for the 6-hour window (12:00–18:00) that determines whether a pilgrim's Hajj is valid at all.

The design

  • Offline GPS boundary check: polygon stored on-chip, checked every 10 seconds, no internet needed.
  • 5-state haptic language: Normal → Advisory → Warning → Danger → Emergency, each with a distinct vibration pattern and LED color, taught in a 2-minute pre-Hajj tutorial.
  • Group leader dashboard: live map of all pilgrims in a group, color-coded by state, with automatic escalation to Hajj Authority and medical staff.
  • Privacy-first: GPS deleted after Hajj, vitals retained 72 hours, full transparency on what's collected and why.

Evaluation

Evaluated across all 13 screens using Nielsen's 10 Heuristics and a cognitive walkthrough of the primary flow. The system scored strongly across visibility of status, error prevention, and minimalist design, with the main opportunity flagged around in-app documentation for haptic pattern recall.

Najah's role

Contributed to a 5-person team at Umm Al-Qura University (HCI Research, 2025–2026) under Dr. Hnan Hayat, working on context definition, human state analysis, and interaction design across the mobile and wristband experiences.
