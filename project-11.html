Overview

A heuristic usability evaluation of two real, live Saudi charitable-giving platforms: Ehsan (website + app, developed by SDAIA) and Tabaru (a Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development platform). Both connect donors to vetted charitable causes, aligned with Saudi Vision 2030's push for transparent, accessible giving.

What this project taught me

  • Applying Jakob Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics rigorously to live, production platforms rather than in-class mockups.
  • Prioritizing and severity-rating real issues (0–4 scale) instead of just listing observations.
  • Scoping evaluation tasks precisely: donating to a project, viewing records, cancelling a donation.
  • Using the Nielsen Norman Group Heuristic Evaluation Workbook format for structured, evidence-based findings.

Usability goals applied

Effectiveness, efficiency, safety, simplicity, learnability, memorability, and future development — each defined concretely against real donation-flow examples (e.g. verifying donor details before payment, supporting future cryptocurrency donations).

Key findings — Ehsan

  • User Control & Freedom (4/4): no "Undo" after actions like modifying data — recommended an Undo button on major operations.
  • Visibility of System Status (3/4): no progress indicator during donation processing — recommended a loading/progress state.
  • Error Prevention (3/4): no confirmation before critical actions like donations — recommended a confirmation dialog.
  • Recognition Over Recall (3/4): "Donation Records" buried in menus — recommended surfacing it on the homepage.

Key findings — Tabaru

  • User Control & Freedom (4/4): no way to retrieve or cancel a completed donation — recommended an emergency-retrieval request flow.
  • Visibility of System Status (3/4): no clear status shown during donations or page loads.
  • Help & Documentation (3/4): limited, hard-to-find FAQ — recommended a prominent, searchable Help section.
  • Aesthetic & Minimalist Design (2/4): disorganized homepage — recommended prioritizing the most-needed charitable projects.

Najah's role

Contributed to task scoping, heuristic analysis, and the structured NN/G-format write-up as part of a 5-person team, cross-checking findings between the Ehsan and Tabaru evaluations for consistency.
