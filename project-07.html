Overview

UX research project on Jahez, one of Saudi Arabia's most popular food delivery apps. The team chose Jahez over HungerStation and ToYou for its familiarity, ease of use, and popularity, then focused on a single high-friction moment: the app has no way to cancel an order once it's placed.

What this project taught me

  • Choosing and justifying a research target based on real usage patterns, not assumptions.
  • Task analysis: turning a single pain point into a concrete design fix (a 15-minute cancel window with refund).
  • Building 3 distinct personas and mapping each one's emotional journey through the same broken flow.
  • Translating research into prioritized, testable user stories.

The chosen problem

When a user tries to cancel an order on Jahez, there is no cancel button anywhere in the flow. The team's task analysis proposed a fix: let users cancel up to 15 minutes after placing an order, with an immediate refund if they do.

Personas & journeys

  • Sarah, 30, teacher — orders food to arrive at school before her break; gets stuck when she can't cancel a delayed order.
  • Ahmed, 19, student — orders while studying, makes a mistake, can't cancel or get a refund.
  • Laila, 40, vocational guide for people with disabilities — struggles to cancel and finds the app hard to handle overall.

Each persona's journey was mapped across Awareness → App Search → Ordering & Waiting → Receiving, tracking actions, thoughts, and emotions at every stage — consistently ending in frustration at the missing cancel button.

User stories

  • As a user, I'd like the option to cancel my order post-creation, so I can change my mind.
  • As a user, I'd like to be informed in advance if there is no cancellation feature.
  • As a user, I want the freedom to cancel or modify my order within 15 minutes and receive a refund if I cancel.

Storyboard

A 5-panel storyboard illustrates Ahmed's experience: getting hungry while studying, ordering from Jahez, forgetting he had somewhere to be, discovering there's no cancel button, and feeling shocked that he can't get his money back.

Najah's role

Co-led this project with a 4-person team at Umm Al-Qura University. Najah contributed to the scope definition, persona development, and journey mapping — building skills in translating a single observed pain point into a structured, evidence-backed case for a feature.
