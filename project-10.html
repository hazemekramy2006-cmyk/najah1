Overview

The design-and-research counterpart to The Ingredients App project plan — same product concept, different lens. This project took the idea from a 13-response survey through low-fidelity sketches, mid-fidelity wireframes, a polished high-fidelity prototype, and real usability testing with two age groups.

What this project taught me

  • Running and analyzing a user survey to validate assumptions before designing anything.
  • Progressing a design through three fidelity stages — sketch → wireframe → high-fidelity — without skipping steps.
  • Usability testing across different age groups and translating timed task data into concrete design recommendations.
  • Documenting assumptions explicitly and pairing each one with a design decision that resolves it.

Research findings

  • 69.2% of respondents said they "sometimes" find themselves missing ingredients when cooking.
  • Lack of time (61.5%) and not knowing what to cook (38.5%) were the top cooking challenges.
  • 92.3% wanted a feature to flag allergies or chronic conditions for personalized recipes.
  • 69.2% said they'd most likely access a recipe app from a smartphone.

Design process

Paper sketches (Low-Fidelity) established the homepage, login, and ingredients-input flow. These were digitized into grayscale Mid-Fidelity wireframes, then into a full-color High-Fidelity prototype with a warm pink palette, recipe cards, ratings, reviews, and a favorites system — all connected through a mapped user flow from onboarding to recipe detail.

Usability testing results

Tested with a 21-year-old and a 55-year-old user. The younger user reached the ingredients page in 15 seconds and the recipes page in 18 seconds with generally positive feedback. The older user took longer (20s and 28s respectively) and had more difficulty with icons and shortcuts — leading to recommendations for clearer navigation, quick tips, and added back-navigation arrows.
